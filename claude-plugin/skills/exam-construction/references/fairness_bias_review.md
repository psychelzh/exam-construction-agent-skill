# Fairness and Bias Review for Classroom Exams

Use this checklist before finalizing a formal exam or after identifying anomalous item statistics.

## Construct alignment

- Does the item measure the intended course construct rather than reading speed, cultural familiarity, or test-wiseness?
- Is the language demand necessary for the construct?
- Are examples and names incidental rather than essential?
- Would students who learned the target content but lack a particular background still have a fair chance?

## Content and context

- Avoid contexts that require specialized out-of-course knowledge.
- Avoid stereotypes, stigmatizing descriptions, or culturally narrow assumptions.
- Avoid cases where socioeconomic, family, disability, gender, or cultural background is used merely as decoration or a cue.
- When culturally specific cases are necessary, provide all information needed to answer.

## Accommodations

- Consider presentation, response, timing, setting, assistive support, and alternate assessment formats.
- Preserve the construct: do not remove the skill being intentionally assessed.
- Record what accommodation changes and what inference remains defensible.

## Bias evidence after testing

- Mean differences alone do not establish item bias.
- Flag items with unexpected difficulty or discrimination patterns across groups only when group data are adequate and ethically usable.
- Combine statistical evidence with content review, response-process review, and instructional coverage review.
