# Exam Construction Quality Checklists

## Assessment-Claim and Score-Interpretation Checklist

- [ ] The intended inference is explicit.
- [ ] Score interpretation is specified as criterion-referenced, norm-referenced, diagnostic, qualitative, or mixed.
- [ ] The assessment purpose matches the planned item types and score use.
- [ ] The exam score is not asked to support unsupported decisions.

## Blueprint Checklist

- [ ] Assessment purpose is explicit.
- [ ] Content domains are defined from syllabus, lectures, textbook chapters, or teacher-provided priorities.
- [ ] Cognitive levels are specified.
- [ ] Score weights correspond to instructional emphasis and course objectives.
- [ ] Applied objectives are assessed with applied items.
- [ ] Objectives are stated with assessable verbs.
- [ ] Content and cognitive weights correspond to instructional emphasis.
- [ ] The exam does not overrepresent easily testable trivia.

## Selected-Response Checklist

- [ ] Stem poses a complete, focused problem.
- [ ] One and only one option is defensibly best.
- [ ] All distractors are plausible for partially prepared students.
- [ ] Distractors reflect meaningful misconceptions or application errors.
- [ ] Options are homogeneous in type and parallel in form.
- [ ] No grammatical cues, length cues, or keyword cues.
- [ ] No “all/none of the above” or compound options.
- [ ] Negative wording is avoided or justified.
- [ ] Difficulty arises from conceptual discrimination or application, not trick wording.
- [ ] Correct-answer positions are balanced across the exam.
- [ ] True/false items, if used, contain unambiguous statements and avoid trivial negation.
- [ ] Matching items, if used, contain homogeneous premises and responses.

## Short-Answer Checklist

- [ ] Prompt specifies the expected task: define, compare, explain, analyze, evaluate, or apply.
- [ ] Expected answer elements are listed.
- [ ] Point allocation is explicit.
- [ ] Acceptable alternative wording is identified.
- [ ] Common partial-credit patterns are defined.

## Constructed-Response and Case-Analysis Checklist

- [ ] Scenario is realistic and contains sufficient evidence for analysis.
- [ ] Required constructs or theories are taught in the course.
- [ ] Students are asked to justify claims with case evidence.
- [ ] Rubric separates concept identification, explanation, evidence use, reasoning, and implication.
- [ ] No answer depends on unstated facts or hidden assumptions.
- [ ] Restricted-response and extended-response tasks are clearly distinguished.
- [ ] Model answers or anchor responses are prepared before scoring.


## Performance Assessment / Portfolio Checklist

- [ ] The task requires authentic application rather than decorative performance.
- [ ] Task instructions, materials, constraints, and deliverables are explicit.
- [ ] Criteria and performance levels are defined before administration.
- [ ] Procedures reduce rater error and irrelevant presentation effects.
- [ ] Portfolio requirements specify required artifacts, selection rules, reflection, and revision policy.

## Reliability and Validity-Evidence Checklist

- [ ] Important constructs are sampled with enough items or adequate scoring evidence.
- [ ] Subjective items use analytic rubrics and explicit partial-credit rules.
- [ ] Content evidence is documented through the blueprint.
- [ ] Response-process plausibility is checked: students must use the intended reasoning.
- [ ] Consequences and likely washback are considered.

## Fairness, Accommodation, and Bias Checklist

- [ ] Language, cultural knowledge, disability-related access barriers, and reading load are construct-relevant or minimized.
- [ ] Necessary contextual information is provided within the item.
- [ ] Accommodations preserve the construct being assessed.
- [ ] Potential bias is reviewed through content, internal features, administration, and consequence rather than mean differences alone.

## Post-Exam Item Analysis Checklist

- [ ] Item difficulty is computed or estimated.
- [ ] Item discrimination is computed or qualitatively evaluated.
- [ ] Distractor frequencies are reviewed.
- [ ] Negative-discrimination items are checked for miskey, ambiguity, or non-taught content.
- [ ] Results are used to revise items and improve instruction.

## Exam-Level Checklist

- [ ] Total score and time limit are feasible.
- [ ] Reading load is reasonable.
- [ ] Content distribution matches the blueprint.
- [ ] Difficulty distribution is appropriate for the assessment purpose.
- [ ] There is no avoidable redundancy.
- [ ] Answer key and rubric are complete.
- [ ] The paper has been reviewed for ambiguity, fairness, factual accuracy, administration feasibility, and score-interpretation limits.
